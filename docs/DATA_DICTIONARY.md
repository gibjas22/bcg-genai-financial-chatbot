# Data Dictionary

How the extracted 10-K data is structured, and why it is shaped this way for the AI chatbot.

## Structure

**Tidy ("long") format: one row per company per fiscal year.** Each column holds one variable and each row one observation. This lets the chatbot answer any question with a simple lookup (company + year + metric) and means new companies, years or quarterly 10-Q data are added as rows without changing any code.

## `data/financial_data.xlsx` and `data/financial_data.csv` (raw extraction)

The figures were compiled manually into the Excel spreadsheet, then converted to CSV in the notebook for pandas. Both hold the same nine rows: three companies × fiscal years 2023 to 2025.

| Column | Type | Unit | Source in 10-K | Description |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Company | text | n/a | 10-K cover page | Company name: Microsoft, Tesla or Apple |
| Fiscal Year | integer | year | 10-K cover page | Fiscal year the figures relate to |
| Fiscal Year End | date (YYYY-MM-DD) | n/a | 10-K cover page | Last day of the fiscal year. Ends differ by company, so years are not calendar-aligned |
| Total Revenue | number | US$ millions | Item 8: Income statement | Total net sales or revenues for the year |
| Net Income | number | US$ millions | Item 8: Income statement | Bottom-line profit. Tesla: net income attributable to common stockholders |
| Total Assets | number | US$ millions | Item 8: Balance sheet | Everything the company owns, at fiscal year end |
| Total Liabilities | number | US$ millions | Item 8: Balance sheet | Everything the company owes, at fiscal year end |
| Cash Flow from Operating Activities | number | US$ millions | Item 8: Cash flow statement | Cash generated by day-to-day operations |

## `data/financial_analysis.csv` (chatbot input)

All columns above, plus these derived fields:

| Column | Unit | Formula | What it tells you |
|---|---|---|---|
| `Revenue Growth (%)`, `Net Income Growth (%)`, `Assets Growth (%)`, `Liabilities Growth (%)`, `Operating Cash Flow Growth (%)` | % | (This year ÷ last year − 1) × 100, per company | Year-on-year change. Blank for each company's first year (FY2023) |
| Net Margin (%) | % | Net Income ÷ Total Revenue × 100 | Profitability: profit kept from each dollar of sales |
| Liabilities to Assets (%) | % | Total Liabilities ÷ Total Assets × 100 | Leverage: share of assets funded by liabilities |
| OCF to Net Income (x) | ratio | Operating Cash Flow ÷ Net Income | Earnings quality: above 1.0 means profit is backed by cash |
| Shareholders Equity | US$ millions | Total Assets − Total Liabilities | What the owners hold after all obligations (the balance sheet identity) |

## `data/financial_features_ml.csv` (machine learning ready)

All columns from `financial_analysis.csv`, plus:

| Column | Type | Description |
|---|---|---|
| `<Metric> (Prior Year)` | number | Lag feature: the same company's value for the previous fiscal year (revenue, net income, operating cash flow). Blank for the first year |
| `Company_Apple`, `Company_Microsoft`, `Company_Tesla` | 0/1 | One-hot encoding of the company name, so models can use it as a numeric input |

**Preprocessing choices:** figures stay in nominal US$ millions, as reported, to keep them traceable to the 10-K (inflation does not change any three-year trend or ranking here). Seasonality does not apply to annual data, but will once quarterly 10-Q data is added.

## Where the data and its context come from

Following the SEC investor bulletin [How to Read a 10-K/10-Q](https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/general-resources/news-alerts/alerts-bulletins/investor-bulletins/how-read):

| 10-K section | Used for |
|---|---|
| **Item 8: Financial Statements and Supplementary Data** | Every figure in the dataset (audited statements) |
| **Item 7: Management's Discussion and Analysis (MD&A)** | Explaining *why* figures moved, e.g. Tesla's one-off FY2023 tax benefit and Microsoft's Activision Blizzard acquisition |
| **Item 1 and Item 1A: Business and Risk Factors** | Understanding each company's model before interpreting its numbers |

Quarterly 10-Q filings follow the same pattern (Financial Statements, then MD&A) and are the natural next data source.

## Data quality rules (checked in the notebook)

1. No missing values in any metric.
2. No duplicate company and fiscal year rows.
3. All metrics stored as numbers, in US$ millions.
4. Exactly three fiscal years per company (2023, 2024, 2025).
5. Total Liabilities below Total Assets in every row.
6. Every figure traceable to a named statement in Item 8 of the 10-K.
